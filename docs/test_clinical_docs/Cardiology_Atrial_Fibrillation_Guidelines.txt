1. Executive Summary & Clinical Scope
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common sustained cardiac arrhythmia globally, associated with a five-fold increase in the risk of stroke, a three-fold increase in heart failure risk, and elevated cardiovascular mortality. This clinical guideline delineates evidence-based diagnostic criteria, stroke risk stratification algorithms, pharmacologic rate vs. rhythm control strategies, and anticoagulation therapy recommendations.

2. Diagnostic Criteria & Initial Assessment
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
A definitive diagnosis of atrial fibrillation requires standard 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) documentation or a single-lead ECG tracing showing an irregular rhythm without discernible P waves lasting at least 30 seconds.
Initial clinical workup must include:
• 12-lead ECG assessing ventricular rate, QRS width, and ischemic changes.
• Transthoracic Echocardiogram (TTE) evaluating left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), left atrial dimension, and valvular heart disease.
• Laboratory panel: Thyroid stimulating hormone (TSH), complete blood count, serum creatinine, eGFR, liver transaminases, and electrolytes.

3. Thromboembolism Risk Stratification (CHA2DS2-VASc)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Oral anticoagulation decisions are guided by the validated CHA2DS2-VASc score:
• Congestive heart failure / LV dysfunction (+1 point)
• Hypertension consistently >140/90 mmHg or treated (+1 point)
• Age >= 75 years (+2 points)
• Diabetes mellitus (+1 point)
• Stroke, TIA, or prior thromboembolism (+2 points)
• Vascular disease (prior MI, peripheral artery disease, aortic plaque) (+1 point)
• Age 65 to 74 years (+1 point)
• Sex category Female (+1 point)

Clinical Recommendations:
- Score >= 2 in men or >= 3 in women: Oral anticoagulation is strongly recommended (Class I, Level A).
- Score = 1 in men or = 2 in women: Oral anticoagulation should be considered based on bleeding risk and shared decision-making.
- Score = 0 in men or = 1 in women: Anticoagulation is generally omitted (Class III: Harm).

4. Oral Anticoagulation Pharmacotherapy
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Direct Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs) are first-line agents over vitamin K antagonists (Warfarin) for non-valvular AF due to superior safety profiles and significantly reduced intracranial hemorrhage risk.

Specific Dosing Guidelines:
• Apixaban (Eliquis): Standard dose is 5 mg PO twice daily (BID). Reduce dose to 2.5 mg PO BID if the patient meets at least TWO of the following ABC criteria: Age >= 80 years, Body weight <= 60 kg, or Serum creatinine >= 1.5 mg/dL.
• Rivaroxaban (Xarelto): Standard dose is 20 mg PO once daily taken with the evening meal. Dose reduced to 15 mg daily if CrCl is 15-49 mL/min.
• Dabigatran (Pradaxa): 150 mg PO twice daily. Consider 110 mg PO BID in patients >= 80 years or with elevated bleeding risk.
• Warfarin: Indicated specifically in mechanical heart valves or moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis. Target INR range is 2.0 to 3.0 (2.5 to 3.5 for mechanical mitral valves).

5. Ventricular Rate Control Strategies
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
For hemodynamically stable AF, ventricular rate control is initial management. Target resting heart rate is < 110 bpm (lenient control) or < 80 bpm if symptomatic.

First-line Pharmacotherapy:
• Beta-Blockers:
  - Metoprolol succinate: 50 mg to 200 mg PO once daily.
  - Bisoprolol: 2.5 mg to 10 mg PO once daily.
  - Carvedilol: 3.125 mg to 25 mg PO twice daily (preferred in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction).
• Non-Dihydropyridine Calcium Channel Blockers:
  - Diltiazem: 120 mg to 360 mg PO daily (avoid in HFrEF with LVEF < 40%).
  - Verapamil: 120 mg to 360 mg PO daily in divided doses.
• Second-line Add-on:
  - Digoxin: 0.0625 mg to 0.25 mg daily, useful in sedentary patients with concurrent HFrEF.
  - Amiodarone: Reserved for refractory rate control or when other agents are contraindicated.

6. Rhythm Control & Cardioversion Protocol
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Rhythm control is indicated for patients with persistent symptoms despite rate control, younger patients, or heart failure precipitated by tachycardia.

Cardioversion Anticoagulation Protocol:
• If AF duration > 48 hours (or unknown duration): Must complete at least 3 consecutive weeks of therapeutic anticoagulation before elective cardioversion, OR undergo transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) to exclude left atrial appendage (LAA) thrombus.
• Post-cardioversion: Therapeutic anticoagulation must be maintained for a minimum of 4 weeks regardless of CHA2DS2-VASc score.
